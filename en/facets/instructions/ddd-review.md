Focus on reviewing **domain-driven design**: the domain model declaration, the code's fidelity to it, the domain, use-case, and interface adapter layer rules, the package structure, and the language rules.
Do not assume another reviewer or step has already covered an issue. Detect any problem that belongs to this review perspective.

- Read `.ddd.toml` and the files under `docs/ddd/` as this change leaves them. Check the model elements, mapping rows, and layer structure entries the change adds or changes.
- For each changed domain, use-case, and interface adapter source, compare it with its mapping row: package, module path, type, methods of the mapped operations, error types and cases, replay methods, and restoration path.
- Check the module placement and package names of every package the change touches, including files the change did not edit but made unreachable or misplaced.
- Review Module meaning against the `domain_packages` rationale and the code: business cohesion, whether each Module can be understood without detailed knowledge of others, fragmentation by technical classification, and cycles between peer Modules. Assess the need for parent-child dependencies. Several Aggregates in one Module are not a finding by themselves; check that each Aggregate boundary is preserved. Check business reasons for Module splits, merges, renames, and added Contexts. A passing automated check alone does not establish conceptual cohesion.
- Review model validity separately from declaration fidelity. Use requirement examples, counterexamples, and client tests to assess whether responsibilities and language explain the business. Check that discoveries from implementation reach declarations, mappings, and tests; when the model must change, state the evidence and replanning scope.
- Compare the meanings of changed terms, Context boundaries and relationships, and adoption, sharing, or translation responsibilities with the plan and code. Matching names alone do not prove matching meaning. Check that unconfirmed business rules are not presented as facts.
- Check Aggregate boundaries against immediate invariants and cross-Aggregate rules against tolerated delays and intermediate states. Detect atomic guarantees attributed only to Process Managers or compensation. Check Entity/Value classification rationale, equality, and the scope of stable identity. Differences from the project's explicit language or persistence choices alone are not findings.
- For a changed Domain Service, compare "Domain Service Decisions", existing model references, and placement rationale with the code. Check whether the operation naturally belongs to an existing Entity or Value, whether it displaces their decisions, and whether earlier invocations affect its decisions. Check for loading, persistence, external communication, or use-case coordination mixed into it. Do not infer statefulness from fields alone or treat a plan or Module record as resolving a missing independent-operation declaration.
- Name the violated rule and the model ID or mapping entry involved in each finding.
- Do not read under `.takt/` other than the reports of this run: the DDD rules are in the policies and knowledge given to this step. This step is read-only and cannot run ddd-lint; take its result from the implementation report, where the coder records it, and review what ddd-lint does not check (the meaning of the model and the code).
